Results · model comparison

Which model actually wins?

Trained on 2002–2018 (153 rows), tested on 2019–2023 (45 rows) — years the model never saw. Sorted by test RMSE, lower is better.

Honest read: the naive "yield repeats last year" baseline is already strong (R²=0.954) — most of a country's cereal yield is just its recent level. The best model (a country fixed-effects regression, tied with Random Forest) beats it by only ~13% RMSE. A real, disclosed lift — not a headline number. LightGBM's stock hyperparameters (min_child_samples=20) are tuned for far more rows than this study's 153; no tuning was done to flatter it.
Results · feature importance

What the winning model actually looks at

XGBoost feature importance — season-average NDVI and last year's own yield carry nearly all of it.

A single weather point per country (season_precip_total_mm + season_temp_mean_c) contributes under 2% combined — consistent with using one grain-belt coordinate instead of a country-wide average. This is exactly why the scenario simulator below resamples real NDVI/weather together, rather than moving rainfall on its own.

Known limitations

Proxy label

Cereal yield, not wheat-specific. World Bank's series covers all cereals; FAOSTAT's wheat-only series was down (HTTP 521) while this was built. Defensible for Afghanistan (>80% wheat), less so elsewhere in the panel.

Fixed window

Same Feb–Jun season for every country, not a per-country wheat calendar. Approximate, not phenology-matched.

Spatial blend

Country-level satellite averaging mixes non-cropland pixels — desert, mountains, cities — diluting the signal vs. a cropland mask.

Point weather

One coordinate per country, not an area average — NASA POWER is a point API. See the <2% combined feature importance above.

Small N

~150 training rows. Enough to compare models honestly, not enough for strong generalization claims. Simulator pools go as low as 8 seasons.

No field data

Labels are national statistics, not farmer-reported outcomes — nowhere in this pipeline is there ground truth from an actual field.
